Deltempo interface sounds
=========================

Provenance : original work, generated by scripts/generate_sounds.ps1
License    : none required - the four cues are synthesised for this project, not
             sampled from a third-party pack. There is no upstream audio to credit.

Files in this folder, and the recipe that produces them:

  click.wav    45 ms   1250 Hz + 2500 Hz partial + filtered noise transient
  success.wav  250 ms  C6 (1046.50 Hz) -> G6 (1567.98 Hz), ascending fifth
  error.wav    210 ms  Eb4 (311.13 Hz) -> Bb3 (233.08 Hz), descending fourth
  warning.wav  300 ms  G5 (783.99 Hz) twice, the "finished, but check this" beep

Why synthesised
---------------
The earlier set was four unrelated samples from a public pack. They had been chosen
by measurement - shortest, darkest, most distinguishable - but they were composed by
four different hands, so they did not sound like one product. They also had two
audible defects that no amount of level matching fixes:

  * the click peaked in the first millisecond at a ~132 Hz fundamental, which is
    below what a laptop speaker reproduces, so the most-heard cue in the app was
    mostly a puff of air;
  * the click and the error cue both started and stopped on a non-zero sample,
    i.e. on a step, which is a click in the literal sense.

Synthesising the family fixes both and makes the mix tunable in code. Every cue now
shares one voice:

  * raised-cosine attack (1.2 ms for the tick, 4 ms for the notes), never a step;
  * additive partials whose higher harmonics decay faster than the fundamental, the
    way a struck body settles;
  * a 12 ms cosine fade at the end, so nothing is ever truncated while moving;
  * DC removed, peak written at 0.75, then normalised per cue at load time.

Design rules that still apply
-----------------------------
  * click is the shortest and quietest cue (target 0.50), because it fires on nearly
    every interaction and must stay in the background;
  * success and error are an interval apart, not just a level apart: an ascending
    fifth against a descending fourth, and error is deliberately dull (odd harmonics
    around 233-311 Hz) so the two are told apart by ear alone;
  * warning repeats one mid note instead of resolving, which reads as "look at this"
    rather than "done";
  * nothing is longer than 300 ms, so a stream of interactions never queues up.

Regenerating
------------
    pwsh -File scripts/generate_sounds.ps1

The script is deterministic (fixed PRNG seed, fixed sample rate), so a regenerated
file only changes in git when the recipe changes. Files are mono 44.1 kHz 16-bit PCM;
SoundService rejects anything else at load time.

Levels are normalised in SoundService at load time, so the four cues sit at a
consistent level regardless of how they were written.
